### external-ear
- latinName: Auris externa
- summary: The auricle (pinna, elastic cartilage covered by skin that is tightly adherent anteriorly, with a cartilage-free lobule), the external acoustic meatus and the lateral surface of the tympanic membrane. The meatus is about 2.5 cm long and S-shaped; its outer third is cartilaginous (hairs, sebaceous and ceruminous glands) and its inner two thirds are bony. The auricle forms from six hillocks of the first and second pharyngeal arches and the meatus from the first pharyngeal cleft.
- location: On the side of the head, with the auricle between the level of the eyebrow and the base of the nose. The meatus lies in front of the mastoid and behind the temporomandibular joint and parotid gland, running medially with a slight upward then downward course.
- function: Collects and funnels sound, with canal resonance amplifying about 2 to 4 kHz, and helps to localise sound. Cerumen protects the canal. Sensory supply: auriculotemporal nerve (V3) anteriorly, great auricular (C2 to C3) to the lobule and lower ear, lesser occipital (C2) to the upper posterior ear, and the auricular branch of the vagus (Arnold nerve) to the floor of the meatus and part of the concha.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: posterior auricular and superficial temporal arteries (external carotid), with deep auricular and occipital branches. Venous: superficial temporal and posterior auricular veins to the retromandibular and external jugular veins. Lymph: preauricular (parotid), postauricular (mastoid) and infra-auricular nodes, then deep cervical nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Otitis externa (Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus; malignant otitis externa in diabetic and immunocompromised patients), cerumen impaction, foreign bodies and auricular haematoma (leading to cauliflower ear if not drained) are common. Stimulating the meatus can provoke the Arnold nerve cough reflex or vasovagal syncope. For otoscopy the auricle is pulled up and back in adults (back and down in young children). Referred otalgia arises from teeth, the temporomandibular joint and the pharynx (V3, IX and X). Preauricular sinuses and microtia or atresia are congenital anomalies.
