### trachea
- latinName: Trachea
- summary: A fibrocartilaginous airway about 10 to 12 cm long and 2 cm in diameter, kept patent by 16 to 20 incomplete C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings and closed posteriorly by the trachealis (smooth muscle) and fibroelastic membrane. It is lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells over a seromucous-gland-containing lamina propria. It arises from the laryngotracheal groove of the endodermal foregut in the fourth week of development.
- location: Extends from the lower border of the cricoid cartilage (C6) to the carina at about T4 to T5 (the sternal angle in quiet expiration), descending slightly to the right. Anteriorly in the neck: the isthmus of the thyroid (over rings 2 to 4), the inferior thyroid veins and the sternohyoid and sternothyroid; in the thorax: the left brachiocephalic vein, the brachiocephalic trunk, the aortic arch, the thymic remnant and the manubrium. Posteriorly: the esophagus, with the left recurrent laryngeal nerve in the tracheo-oesophageal groove. Laterally: the lobes of the thyroid, common carotid arteries, and in the thorax the arch of the azygos vein and right vagus nerve (right) and the aortic arch and left common carotid and subclavian arteries (left).
- function: Conducts, warms and humidifies inspired air; the mucociliary escalator moves trapped particles toward the larynx. Trachealis contraction narrows the lumen during coughing, increasing air velocity. Parasympathetic secretomotor and bronchomotor fibres come from the vagus via the recurrent laryngeal nerves; sympathetic fibres come from the sympathetic trunk; the cough reflex is mediated by vagal afferents.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: inferior thyroid arteries (cervical part) and bronchial arteries (thoracic part). Venous: inferior thyroid venous plexus to the left brachiocephalic vein. Lymph: pretracheal and paratracheal nodes, then the bronchomediastinal trunks.
- clinicalNotes: Tracheostomy is usually performed through rings 2 to 4, below the thyroid isthmus; the thyroidea ima artery and the left brachiocephalic vein (in children) are hazards. A midline neck mass moving on swallowing suggests thyroid enlargement; retrosternal goitre can compress the trachea. Tracheal deviation points to tension pneumothorax (away from the side), or collapse and fibrosis (toward the side). Tracheo-oesophageal fistula and oesophageal atresia (most commonly type C) present with polyhydramnios, choking and aspiration. Post-intubation or post-tracheostomy stenosis, tracheomalacia and tracheal tumours cause stridor. Widening of the carinal angle on chest imaging suggests subcarinal lymphadenopathy or left atrial enlargement.

### right-main-bronchus-proper
- latinName: Bronchus principalis dexter
- summary: The right principal bronchus, about 2.5 cm long before its first division, wider (about 1.4 cm) and more vertical (about 25 degrees from the midline) than the left. Wall structure follows the trachea, with cartilage plates replacing the C-shaped rings. Derived from the right lung bud of the foregut endoderm.
- location: Passes from the carina at T4 to T5, inferolaterally to the right hilum at about T5 to T6, lying below the arch of the azygos vein. The right upper lobe (eparterial) bronchus arises from it about 2.5 cm below the carina, above the level of the right pulmonary artery. Posterior to it lies the oesophagus; anteriorly lie the right pulmonary artery and ascending aorta.
- function: Ventilates the right lung. Vagal parasympathetic fibres cause bronchoconstriction; sympathetic fibres from T2 to T5 cause bronchodilation; the pulmonary plexus is the relay.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: a single right bronchial artery, usually from the third posterior intercostal artery (intercostobronchial trunk) or the aorta. Venous: bronchial veins to the azygos vein (extrapulmonary) and to pulmonary veins (intrapulmonary). Lymph: bronchopulmonary (hilar) nodes, then tracheobronchial and right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Aspirated foreign bodies and endotracheal tubes that are inserted too deep enter the right bronchus more often because it is wider and straighter; selective right-sided intubation leaves the left lung unventilated. Squamous cell and small-cell carcinomas often arise centrally here and cause collapse, post-obstructive pneumonia or haemoptysis. Compression by lymph nodes or tumour can mimic asthma (monophonic wheeze).

### left-main-bronchus
- latinName: Bronchus principalis sinister
- summary: The left principal bronchus, about 5 cm long, narrower (about 1.0 cm) and more horizontal (about 45 degrees) than the right, giving the upper and lower lobar bronchi (the left upper lobe bronchus arises within the hilum, hyparterial).
- location: Passes below the aortic arch, anterior to the oesophagus, descending aorta and thoracic duct, and behind the left pulmonary artery, which arches over it. Reaches the left hilum at about T6. The left recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks around the aortic arch just above it.
- function: Ventilates the left lung. Autonomic control is as for the right (pulmonary plexus).
- bloodSupply: Arterial: two left bronchial arteries, directly from the descending thoracic aorta. Venous: bronchial veins to the accessory hemiazygos vein or left superior intercostal vein. Lymph: bronchopulmonary nodes, then tracheobronchial nodes and the left, and to a lesser extent right, paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Left atrial enlargement (for example in mitral stenosis) can elevate and compress the left main bronchus, and aortic arch aneurysm can compress it, causing wheeze and left recurrent laryngeal palsy (Ortner syndrome gives hoarseness). Bronchoscopy shows a longer, narrower left main bronchus and the carina as a sharp ridge; a splayed, rigid carina suggests subcarinal nodal disease.

### right-apical-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis apicalis (B1) dexter
- summary: The segmental (tertiary) bronchus and its subdivisions for bronchopulmonary segment S1 of the right upper lobe. Each bronchopulmonary segment is a pyramidal unit with its own segmental bronchus and pulmonary artery branch centrally, and with intersegmental septa containing pulmonary vein tributaries. It develops from the lobar bud by the seventh week.
- location: At the top of the right upper lobe, beneath the dome of the pleura (cupula), behind the first rib and clavicle; the subclavian vessels and the lower trunk of the brachial plexus lie against the cervical pleura nearby. The bronchus is the highest of the three upper-lobe segmental branches.
- function: Ventilates S1 and drains in postural drainage when the patient sits upright. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arterial branches for the airway wall; the apical branch of the truncus anterior of the right pulmonary artery for gas exchange. Venous: intersegmental tributaries to the superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: intrapulmonary nodes, then hilar and right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Typical site of post-primary (reactivation) tuberculosis with cavitation. Apical tumours (Pancoast) invade the brachial plexus and sympathetic chain, causing shoulder and arm pain and Horner syndrome. Segmental anatomy allows segmentectomy for small peripheral cancers.

### right-posterior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis posterior (B2) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S2 of the right upper lobe, with its pulmonary arterial branch and intersegmental veins.
- location: Posterior part of the right upper lobe, beneath ribs 2 to 4 posteriorly, bounded below by the oblique fissure; it lies against the posterior chest wall in the paravertebral region.
- function: Ventilates S2. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries for the wall; the ascending (posterior) branch of the right pulmonary artery for gas exchange. Venous: superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: intrapulmonary and hilar nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Aspiration in a supine patient commonly involves S2 (and the superior segment of the lower lobes). It is a frequent site of tuberculosis and of aspiration pneumonia in the unconscious or bed-bound patient.

### right-anterior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis anterior (B3) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S3 of the right upper lobe, below the apical segment and in front of the posterior segment.
- location: Anterior part of the right upper lobe behind the second to fourth costal cartilages, above the horizontal fissure, which separates it from the middle lobe below.
- function: Ventilates S3. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries for the wall; the anterior segmental branch of the truncus anterior (right pulmonary artery). Venous: superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Collapse of the right upper lobe elevates the horizontal fissure and shifts the trachea to the right; the fissure and the segment border are visible on a frontal radiograph. Pneumonia confined above the horizontal fissure suggests involvement of S3.

### lateral-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis lateralis (B4) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S4, the lateral segment of the right middle lobe. The right middle lobe corresponds to the lingula of the left lung.
- location: Lateral part of the right middle lobe, between the horizontal and oblique fissures, behind the fourth to sixth ribs in the axilla; the middle lobe bronchus arises anteriorly from the intermedius bronchus.
- function: Ventilates S4. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the middle lobe branch of the interlobar right pulmonary artery. Venous: the middle lobe vein, joining the superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: The middle lobe bronchus is long, narrow and surrounded by hilar lymph nodes, which makes it prone to extrinsic compression, collapse and recurrent infection (middle lobe syndrome). It is a common site for bronchiectasis and for atypical mycobacterial infection.

### medial-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis medialis (B5) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S5, the medial segment of the right middle lobe.
- location: Anteromedial part of the right middle lobe, lying against the right atrium and right border of the heart, behind the fourth to sixth costal cartilages.
- function: Ventilates S5. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the middle lobe branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: middle lobe vein to the right superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Consolidation or collapse obscures the right heart border on a frontal radiograph (the silhouette sign); the lateral view shows a triangular density above the oblique fissure. The same sign applies on the left to the lingula.

### right-superior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis superior (B6) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S6, the superior (apical) segment of the right lower lobe, which sits at the top of the lower lobe.
- location: Posterior, beneath the oblique fissure at about the level of the T4 to T7 vertebrae, behind the oesophagus and the hilum. The bronchus arises from the posterior wall of the lower lobe bronchus, directed backwards.
- function: Ventilates S6. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the superior segmental branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Because the bronchus runs backwards, aspirated material enters it when the patient is supine; it is the most common site of aspiration lung abscess.

### right-medial-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis medialis (B7) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S7, the medial basal (cardiac) segment of the right lower lobe.
- location: Medial and inferior part of the right lower lobe, next to the right atrium and the inferior vena cava, resting on the diaphragm and the pulmonary ligament.
- function: Ventilates S7. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the medial basal branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, pulmonary ligament nodes, then subcarinal and paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Disease here lies against the lower right heart border and inferior vena cava. Drainage along the pulmonary ligament means that basal tumours can involve inferior pulmonary ligament nodes (station 9) in staging.

### right-anterior-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis anterior (B8) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S8, the anterior basal segment of the right lower lobe.
- location: Anterior and inferior part of the right lower lobe, resting on the anterior part of the diaphragm and facing the costal surface at the level of ribs 6 to 8.
- function: Ventilates S8. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the anterior basal branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar and pulmonary ligament nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Involved in lobar pneumonia and basal atelectasis after abdominal or thoracic surgery. Right subphrenic pathology (abscess, hepatic disease) can cause adjacent basal collapse or reactive effusion.

### right-lateral-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis lateralis (B9) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S9, the lateral basal segment of the right lower lobe.
- location: Lateral part of the base of the right lower lobe, beneath ribs 7 to 9 in the midaxillary line, adjacent to the costodiaphragmatic recess.
- function: Ventilates S9. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the lateral basal branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Pleural fluid of about 200 ml or more blunts the costophrenic angle on an erect frontal radiograph next to this region; a lateral decubitus film detects smaller volumes. It is a common site for basal consolidation in upright aspiration.

### right-posterior-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis posterior (B10) dexter
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S10, the posterior basal segment of the right lower lobe, the most dependent segment of the lung in both supine and upright positions.
- location: Posteroinferior part of the right lower lobe, resting on the posterior part of the diaphragm and lying in the paravertebral gutter, reaching the posterior costodiaphragmatic recess down to the level of T12.
- function: Ventilates S10. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the posterior basal branch of the right pulmonary artery. Venous: inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar and pulmonary ligament nodes, then right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Aspiration in a sitting or standing patient and gravity-dependent pulmonary oedema or atelectasis typically involve this segment. Because it lies posteriorly, pleural effusions in a seated patient are usually tapped from the back (thoracentesis at about the seventh to ninth intercostal space).

### left-apical-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis apicalis (B1) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S1 of the left upper lobe. On the left it normally arises with B2 as a common apicoposterior bronchus (B1+2).
- location: At the top of the left upper lobe in the cupula of the pleura, behind the first rib and clavicle, with the left subclavian artery and the left brachiocephalic vein medial to it.
- function: Ventilates S1. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries (two left, from the descending aorta); apicoposterior branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then aortopulmonary window and left paratracheal nodes (mostly ipsilateral).
- clinicalNotes: Reactivation tuberculosis, apical tumours with Pancoast syndrome, and apical fibrosis in ankylosing spondylitis or after radiotherapy occur here.

### left-posterior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis posterior (B2) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S2 of the left upper lobe, typically combined with S1 as the apicoposterior segment.
- location: Posterior part of the left upper lobe, behind the aortic arch at about the level of ribs 2 to 4 posteriorly and above the oblique fissure.
- function: Ventilates S2. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; apicoposterior branches of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then aortopulmonary window and left paratracheal nodes (mostly ipsilateral).
- clinicalNotes: Left upper lobe collapse produces a hazy veil-like opacity over the left hemithorax on a frontal view, sometimes with a crescent of aerated, hyperinflated lower lobe around the aortic knuckle (the luftsichel sign), and anterior displacement of the oblique fissure on a lateral view.

### left-anterior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis anterior (B3) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S3 of the left upper lobe.
- location: Anterior part of the left upper lobe behind the second to fourth costal cartilages, above the lingula and in front of the hilum.
- function: Ventilates S3. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the anterior branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then aortopulmonary window and left paratracheal nodes (mostly ipsilateral).
- clinicalNotes: Tumours here may extend to the aortopulmonary window (station 5), which affects staging because the left upper lobe commonly drains to those nodes.

### superior-lingular-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus lingularis superior (B4) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S4, the superior lingular segment of the left upper lobe. The lingula is the embryological and functional equivalent of the right middle lobe.
- location: Anterior and inferior part of the left upper lobe next to the left border of the heart and the cardiac notch, behind the fourth and fifth costal cartilages.
- function: Ventilates S4. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the lingular branch of the left pulmonary artery (superior lingular artery). Venous: left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then aortopulmonary window and left paratracheal nodes (mostly ipsilateral).
- clinicalNotes: Lingular consolidation obscures the left heart border (silhouette sign). Together with the right middle lobe, the lingula is often involved in bronchiectasis and in Mycobacterium avium complex infection.

### inferior-lingular-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus lingularis inferior (B5) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S5, the inferior lingular segment of the left upper lobe.
- location: Lowest part of the lingula, resting on the pericardium and diaphragm near the cardiac apex, at about the level of ribs 5 and 6 anteriorly.
- function: Ventilates S5. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the lingular branch of the left pulmonary artery (inferior lingular artery). Venous: left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then aortopulmonary window and left paratracheal nodes (mostly ipsilateral).
- clinicalNotes: Chronic obstruction here results in lingular collapse or bronchiectasis, with a pattern similar to right middle lobe syndrome.

### left-superior-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis superior (B6) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S6, the superior segment of the left lower lobe.
- location: Posterior and superior part of the left lower lobe, below the oblique fissure, lying against the paravertebral region at the level of about T4 to T7, behind the oesophagus and descending aorta.
- function: Ventilates S6. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the superior segmental branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then paratracheal nodes; lower lobe lymph may cross to the right paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Aspiration pneumonia and lung abscess in supine patients involve S6 on either side, though the right is more common. Lower lobe tumours can spread to contralateral mediastinal nodes, which affects staging.

### left-medial-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis medialis (B7) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S7, the medial basal segment of the left lower lobe, which is usually joined with the anterior basal segment (S7+8, anteromedial basal).
- location: Medial and inferior part of the left lower lobe, behind the heart and near the descending aorta and oesophagus, resting on the diaphragm.
- function: Ventilates S7. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the anteromedial basal branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, pulmonary ligament nodes, then left paratracheal nodes, with possible crossover to the right in lower lobe disease.
- clinicalNotes: Left lower lobe collapse produces a triangular retrocardiac opacity that is visible through the heart shadow (the sail sign) and displaces the oblique fissure posteriorly.

### left-anterior-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis anterior (B8) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S8, the anterior basal segment of the left lower lobe, usually fused with S7.
- location: Anterior and inferior part of the left lower lobe on the diaphragm, behind the fifth and sixth ribs anteriorly.
- function: Ventilates S8. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the anteromedial basal branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes and pulmonary ligament nodes, then left paratracheal nodes, with possible crossover to the right in lower lobe disease.
- clinicalNotes: Basal atelectasis and pneumonia after upper abdominal surgery commonly involve the basal segments.

### left-lateral-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree

- location: Lateral part of the base of the left lower lobe beneath ribs 7 to 9 in the midaxillary line, adjacent to the costodiaphragmatic recess.
- function: Ventilates S9. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the lateral basal branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes, then left paratracheal nodes, with possible crossover to the right in lower lobe disease.

### left-posterior-basal-segmental-bronchial-tree
- latinName: Bronchus segmentalis basalis posterior (B10) sinister
- summary: The segmental bronchus and subdivisions of S10, the posterior basal segment of the left lower lobe, the most dependent lung segment.
- location: Posteroinferior part of the left lower lobe on the posterior diaphragm, in the paravertebral gutter beside the descending aorta, extending to the posterior costodiaphragmatic recess at about T12.
- function: Ventilates S10. Autonomic control is via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: bronchial arteries; the posterior basal branch of the left pulmonary artery. Venous: left inferior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar nodes and pulmonary ligament nodes, then left paratracheal nodes, with possible crossover to the right in lower lobe disease.
- clinicalNotes: Dependent oedema and atelectasis in supine, ventilated patients, and posterior basal pneumonia, commonly present here. It is also a typical site for lung-base fibrosis in usual interstitial pneumonia.

### superior-lingular-artery
- latinName: Arteria lingularis superior
- summary: A segmental branch of the left pulmonary artery, usually arising from the interlobar part (or by a common lingular trunk), which supplies the superior lingular segment (S4). Pulmonary arteries are thin-walled, low-pressure (mean about 15 mmHg), elastic vessels carrying deoxygenated blood.
- location: Runs anterior and slightly inferior to the superior lingular bronchus within the bronchopulmonary segment, ahead of the lingular vein tributaries. The left pulmonary artery arches over the left main bronchus, so the artery is hyparterial in relation to the upper lobe bronchus.
- function: Delivers mixed venous blood from the right ventricle to the alveolar capillaries of S4 for gas exchange. Regional flow is regulated by hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction. Sympathetic nerves (vasoconstriction) reach it via the pulmonary plexus.
- bloodSupply: Own wall: vasa vasorum (from bronchial arteries). Venous return of the segment: the lingular tributary of the left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: the segment drains to hilar and paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Pulmonary emboli lodge in lobar and segmental arteries, producing ventilation-perfusion mismatch; CT pulmonary angiography shows filling defects. Because of the dual (bronchial) supply, only a minority of emboli cause infarction, which is typically wedge-shaped and pleural-based. Chronic embolism and pulmonary hypertension lead to right ventricular strain.

### inferior-lingular-artery
- latinName: Arteria lingularis inferior
- summary: A segmental branch of the left pulmonary artery that supplies the inferior lingular segment (S5), often sharing a common trunk with the superior lingular artery.
- location: Runs with the inferior lingular bronchus, anterior to it, within the lower part of the lingula, near the cardiac apex.
- function: Delivers deoxygenated blood to the alveolar capillaries of S5. It takes part in hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.
- bloodSupply: Own wall: vasa vasorum. Venous return of the segment: the lingular tributary of the left superior pulmonary vein. Lymph: hilar and paratracheal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Segmental emboli can obstruct it. In left upper lobectomy and lingulectomy, identifying the lingular arterial branches is a key step to avoid inadvertent injury or devascularising the lingula.

### diaphragm
- latinName: Diaphragma
- summary: A musculotendinous dome-shaped sheet separating the thoracic and abdominal cavities and the principal muscle of inspiration. It consists of a peripheral skeletal muscle (sternal, costal and lumbar parts) converging on a trefoil central tendon. It develops from the septum transversum, the pleuroperitoneal membranes, the dorsal mesentery of the oesophagus and body wall myotomes (C3 to C5).
- location: Sternal part arises from the posterior xiphoid; costal part from the inner surfaces of the lower six costal cartilages and ribs; lumbar part from the medial and lateral arcuate ligaments and the crura (right crus L1 to L3, left crus L1 to L2). In quiet expiration the right dome reaches about the level of the fifth rib and the left dome is slightly lower, at about the fifth intercostal space. Openings: caval (T8, inferior vena cava and right phrenic branches, in the central tendon), oesophageal (T10, oesophagus, vagal trunks and left gastric vessels, in the right crus), aortic (T12, aorta, thoracic duct and azygos vein, behind the median arcuate ligament). The splanchnic nerves pierce the crura, and the sympathetic trunk passes behind the medial arcuate ligament.
- function: Contraction flattens the domes, increases the vertical thoracic dimension, and with the lower ribs' bucket-handle movement produces inspiration; it contributes to raised intra-abdominal pressure in micturition, defecation and parturition. Motor supply: the phrenic nerve (C3 to C5). Sensory supply: phrenic nerve to the central part; the lower six intercostal nerves and the subcostal nerve to the periphery.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: superior phrenic (thoracic aorta), musculophrenic and pericardiophrenic (internal thoracic), and inferior phrenic arteries (abdominal aorta or coeliac trunk). Venous: corresponding veins to the brachiocephalic, azygos and inferior vena cava systems. Lymph: diaphragmatic nodes, then parasternal, posterior mediastinal and lumbar nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Phrenic nerve injury (for example by mediastinal tumour or surgery) causes an elevated, paradoxically moving hemidiaphragm on fluoroscopy. Irritation of the central diaphragm refers pain to the shoulder tip (C3 to C5 dermatomes), as in subphrenic abscess or ruptured spleen. Hiatal hernias (sliding and paraoesophageal) occur through the oesophageal hiatus. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia most commonly occurs through the posterolateral foramen of Bochdalek (mostly left) and causes pulmonary hypoplasia; the Morgagni hernia is anteromedial. Hiccups result from irritation of the phrenic or vagus nerve.
