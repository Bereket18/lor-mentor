### pituitary-gland
- latinName: Hypophysis (Glandula pituitaria)
- summary: A pea-sized gland (about 1 cm, 0.5 to 0.6 g) with two parts of different origin. The adenohypophysis (anterior lobe, pars tuberalis and pars intermedia) develops from Rathke pouch, an upgrowth of oral ectoderm, and contains somatotrophs, lactotrophs, corticotrophs, thyrotrophs and gonadotrophs. The neurohypophysis (posterior lobe) is a downgrowth of the diencephalon containing pituicytes and axon terminals. The two are joined to the hypothalamus by the infundibulum.
- location: In the hypophyseal fossa of the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone, roofed by the diaphragma sellae. The optic chiasm lies about 1 cm above it; the cavernous sinuses lie laterally and contain the internal carotid artery and cranial nerves III, IV, V1, V2 and VI; the sphenoid sinus lies below, which allows the transsphenoidal surgical route.
- function: The anterior lobe secretes growth hormone, prolactin, ACTH, TSH, FSH and LH under control of hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones delivered by the hypophyseal portal system. The posterior lobe stores and releases antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and oxytocin made in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei and carried down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract. Control is neuroendocrine, with sympathetic fibres from the carotid plexus supplying its vessels.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: superior hypophyseal arteries (internal carotid) supply a primary capillary plexus in the median eminence, which drains through portal veins to a secondary plexus in the anterior lobe; inferior hypophyseal arteries (from the meningohypophyseal trunk) supply the posterior lobe. Venous: cavernous and intercavernous sinuses. No lymphatic vessels are described.
- clinicalNotes: Pituitary adenomas (microadenoma under 10 mm) cause hormone excess (prolactinoma, acromegaly or gigantism, Cushing disease) or mass effect. Compression of the chiasm from below produces bitemporal hemianopia, as the crossing nasal fibres are affected first. Apoplexy is sudden haemorrhage into an adenoma with headache, visual loss and ophthalmoplegia. Sheehan syndrome is postpartum infarction. Hypopituitarism and central diabetes insipidus follow destruction of the gland or stalk; stalk compression raises prolactin through loss of dopamine inhibition.

### left-adrenal-gland
- latinName: Glandula suprarenalis sinistra
- summary: A crescent-shaped retroperitoneal gland of about 4 to 5 g. The cortex (mesodermal, from coelomic epithelium) has three zones: zona glomerulosa (aldosterone), fasciculata (cortisol) and reticularis (androgens). The medulla derives from neural crest and consists of chromaffin cells.
- location: On the anteromedial aspect of the upper pole of the left kidney at about T12 to L1, inside the renal fascia. Anteriorly: lesser sac, stomach, pancreas and splenic vessels. Posteriorly: the left crus and diaphragm. Medially: the left crus and coeliac ganglion.
- function: Cortisol (ACTH-driven, glucose and stress response), aldosterone (renin-angiotensin system, sodium retention) and adrenal androgens. The medulla releases adrenaline and noradrenaline; it is innervated directly by preganglionic sympathetic fibres (T10 to L1, via the splanchnic nerves) that synapse on chromaffin cells with no postganglionic neuron.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: superior (inferior phrenic), middle (aorta) and inferior (renal) suprarenal arteries. Venous: one left adrenal vein, which joins the left inferior phrenic vein and then the left renal vein. Lymph: lateral aortic (para-aortic) nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Cushing syndrome, primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome), Addison disease and adrenal crisis, congenital adrenal hyperplasia (21-hydroxylase deficiency) and phaeochromocytoma (headache, sweating, palpitations, hypertension) are key conditions. Incidentalomas are common; imaging and hormonal testing separate benign adenomas from carcinoma. In adrenal vein sampling for hyperaldosteronism the left vein is easier to cannulate than the right. Neonatal adrenal haemorrhage and neuroblastoma (medullary origin) occur in children.

### right-adrenal-gland
- latinName: Glandula suprarenalis dextra
- summary: A pyramidal (triangular) retroperitoneal gland of about 4 to 5 g with the same cortical zones and neural crest medulla as the left.
- location: Anteromedial to the upper pole of the right kidney at about T12, inside the renal fascia. Anteriorly: the inferior vena cava and the bare area of the right lobe of the liver. Posteriorly: the diaphragm. Medially: the right crus.
- function: Cortisol, aldosterone and androgens from the cortex; catecholamines from the medulla under direct preganglionic sympathetic control.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: superior, middle and inferior suprarenal arteries. Venous: a short (about 1 cm) right adrenal vein that drains directly into the posterior wall of the inferior vena cava. Lymph: lateral aortic (para-aortic) nodes.
- clinicalNotes: The short, wide vein makes right adrenalectomy and venous sampling technically harder, with a risk of caval bleeding. The same hormone excess syndromes occur as on the left. A right adrenal mass can invade the inferior vena cava or liver.
