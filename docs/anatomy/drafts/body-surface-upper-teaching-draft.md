### skin-upper-body
- latinName: Cutis (regio superior corporis)
- summary: The skin of the head, neck, upper limbs and trunk above the umbilicus. It has an epidermis (stratified keratinised squamous epithelium: basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum on thick skin, corneum), a dermis (papillary and reticular layers) and a hypodermis. Cells include keratinocytes, melanocytes (neural crest), Langerhans cells and Merkel cells. The epidermis arises from surface ectoderm and the dermis from mesoderm.
- location: Covers the outer surface of the upper body. Thickness varies from the thin eyelid to the thick skin of the upper back and palms (the only upper-body sites with stratum lucidum are the palms).
- function: Barrier against infection, injury and water loss; thermoregulation through sweating and vasomotor control; touch, pain and temperature sensation; vitamin D synthesis under UVB; immune surveillance. Dermatomal supply: neck C2 to C4, upper limb C5 to T1, nipple T4, umbilicus T10. Sweat glands are supplied by cholinergic sympathetic fibres and arrector pili by noradrenergic ones.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: musculocutaneous and fasciocutaneous perforators from many named arteries (facial, superficial temporal, thyroid, thoracodorsal, intercostal, brachial branches) feeding a subdermal and a dermal plexus. Venous: matching superficial veins to the cephalic, basilic and jugular systems. Lymph: superficial plexus to cervical, supraclavicular and axillary nodes; trunk above the umbilicus drains to axillary nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma (ABCDE criteria, Breslow thickness, sentinel node biopsy to axillary nodes) are major skin cancers. Burns are sized by the rule of nines (head 9 percent, each arm 9, front of trunk 18, back of trunk 18) and fluid resuscitation uses the Parkland formula (4 ml x kg x percent burn). Herpes zoster follows a single dermatome. Langer lines guide incision placement. Cellulitis, eczema, psoriasis and acne are common.
