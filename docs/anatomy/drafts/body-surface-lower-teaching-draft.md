### skin-lower-body
- latinName: Cutis (regio inferior corporis)
- summary: The skin of the lower abdomen, pelvis, buttocks and lower limbs. It has the same layers as elsewhere: epidermis, dermis (papillary and reticular) and hypodermis. The sole has the thickest epidermis with a stratum lucidum; the skin over the inner thigh and groin is thin.
- location: Covers the outer surface of the body from the umbilical level (T10) downward, including the perineum and the foot. Hair follicles and apocrine glands are concentrated in the groin and perineum.
- function: Barrier, thermoregulation and sensation, with particular mechanical protection by the plantar skin. Dermatomal supply: T10 umbilicus, L1 groin, L4 medial leg, L5 dorsum of foot, S1 lateral foot and sole, S2 to S4 perineum. Sweat glands are cholinergic sympathetic.
- bloodSupply: Arterial: perforators from the femoral, profunda femoris, popliteal, tibial and gluteal arteries to a subdermal and a dermal plexus. Venous: great and small saphenous veins, with perforating veins to the deep system. Lymph: superficial inguinal nodes (vertical group for the limb, horizontal group for the lower trunk and perineum), then deep inguinal and external iliac nodes; the lateral foot and posterior calf drain partly to popliteal nodes.
- clinicalNotes: Venous (gaiter area) and arterial ulcers, diabetic foot ulceration and neuropathy, and pressure injuries over the sacrum, heels and greater trochanters are common. Cellulitis, erysipelas, tinea pedis and necrotising fasciitis occur. By the rule of nines each leg (front and back) is 18 percent of body surface, so both legs total 36 percent. Lymphoedema follows lymph node clearance or filariasis. The thigh is a standard donor site for split-thickness skin grafts, and the great saphenous vein is harvested for bypass grafting.
